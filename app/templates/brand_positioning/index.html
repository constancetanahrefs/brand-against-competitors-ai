Same source, no new fetch. Both tabs of this report read one shared store of AI answers. This tab pulls nothing from Brand Radar itself, so adding a report means fetching it in the Sentiment & criticism tab first.

      
Stage 1 — extract claims. For every stored answer that mentions a brand, ±420 characters around each mention (up to 3 excerpts) go to gemini-3-flash in batches of 8. The model returns only explicit positioning claims — "better for backlink analysis", "too expensive for solo users" — each with a verbatim evidence span and any brands it was contrasted against. A bare mention in a list is not a claim. Answers that yield nothing are recorded as read, so they're never re-sent. Cached per (answer, brand).

      
Stage 2 — group semantically. Claim phrases are deduped, embedded (text-embedding-3-small) and merged by cosine similarity, most-common phrasing first so groups are stable across re-runs. Then claude-sonnet labels the groups — it names them, it doesn't decide the grouping. A group needs at least 2 claims to become a category.

      
Why two stages. Extraction is the expensive part and is per-answer, so it caches perfectly. Grouping needs to see all claims at once and is cheap, so you can re-group after a new month — or at a different similarity threshold — without paying to re-read anything.

      
Filtering re-aggregates from claims, not from stored category totals, so a platform- or month-filtered view shows real filtered counts rather than global ones.

      
Read the counts as evidence weight, not market truth. A category with 40 claims means the AI said it 40 times across the answers you track — it is a measure of what the models assert, not of what is actually true about the product.
